# Longitudinal Change

- Follow the numerical evidence: a negative later-minus-earlier difference is a decrease, not an increase.
- Distinguish change in a matched cohort from change between repeated cross-sectional populations.
- Observed change alone does not establish statistical significance. A significance claim requires an appropriate analysis and reported result.
- Observed change or association does not establish causality. A causal claim requires a design and evidence capable of supporting it.
- Numerical direction is not automatically clinical direction. “Improvement” or “worsening” requires verified domain context, a defined desirable direction, and consideration of relevant harms or tradeoffs.
- State the magnitude, units, time points, population, and uncertainty supported by the evidence.
