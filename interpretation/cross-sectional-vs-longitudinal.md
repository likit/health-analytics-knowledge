# Cross-Sectional Versus Longitudinal Analysis

A cross-sectional comparison contrasts populations or samples at one time point, or separate population snapshots at different times. It can describe differences in group-level estimates but does not show how particular individuals changed.

A longitudinal comparison follows the same matched individuals or analytical units over time. It can describe within-unit transitions or changes, subject to matching, attrition, measurement, and design limitations.

Repeated cross-sectional results must not be used to claim that the observed individuals improved, worsened, recovered, or remained persistently abnormal unless individual records were validly linked and analyzed.
